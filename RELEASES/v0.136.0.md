# v0.136.0 — the number that was collected, the place that was not, and the photographs nobody filed

Three things a sealed I-9 should have said and did not. All three were reported
from one form — `I9-2026-0011`, a real hire — and all three turned out to be the
same shape of mistake: **the record held the fact, and the page did not say it.**

The most useful part of this note is the middle one, because it is the only one
where the page said something *false*.

## 1. The SSN box was blank, and the number had been collected

`ssn_last_four` was `8888` on the record. The Desk print format has shown
`XXX-XX-8888` off that column since v0.47.1. The federal page showed nine empty
cells and no explanation, so the operator holding the sealed PDF concluded the
number had never been collected.

It had. Three things had to be true at once for the box to be empty:

1. **The phone throws eight of nine digits away, by design.**
   `OnboardingI9Section1.apiParams` collects all nine — it draws two federal
   forms from them — and sends `ssn_last_four`, with a comment saying a handset
   "cannot read" whether the site wants the whole number, so "sending nine digits
   to find out would be transmitting the most sensitive number on the form on the
   chance the site wanted it." That reasoning is right. **The missing read is
   what forced it**: `store_full_ssn` was reachable only through the MCP
   `get_i9_settings` tool, which no phone calls. So a farm that switched full-SSN
   storage on could never be given a full SSN by the app that collects it, and
   `ssn_full` stayed empty on every phone-filed form on every site.
2. **`generate_i9_pdf` refused `include_full_ssn` by name.**
3. **`_ssn_digits` drops anything that is not nine digits** — correctly; see below.

### The masked option is not available, and the template says so

`XXX-XX-8888` cannot go in that box. Read off the shipped USCIS template:

    'US Social Security Number'   /Ff 29360128   /MaxLen 9
     0x1C00000 = Comb | DoNotSpellCheck | DoNotScroll

A **nine-cell comb with `/MaxLen 9`**. Eleven characters truncate to `XXX-XX-88`.
And `_ssn_digits`'s existing refusal stands for its own reason: five empty cells
followed by `8888` reads as an SSN beginning `0000` to anybody not looking
closely. The comb is unchanged in this release.

### What changed

**The empty box explains itself.** `_ssn_lines` writes into Additional
Information — the box USCIS provides for prose:

> SSN on file: XXX-XX-8888. The box above takes nine digits or none, and this
> site retains four. E-Verify requires the full number: have the employee enter
> it above.

Said **only** when the comb is empty. Where nine digits were printed there is
nothing to explain, and repeating part of the number in a box that does not need
it would put it on the page twice.

**E-Verify changes what a blank box means, so it is in the sentence.** The SSN is
*optional* on Form I-9 and *required* by E-Verify, which submits nine digits and
cannot be run from four. An enrolled employer gets a second clause telling them
the number is still required, and `incomplete` names the box. An employer who
does not use E-Verify is told neither, because for them the blank box is a
complete form and listing it would send them looking for work that does not exist.

**The full-nine path is now reachable end to end.** `generate_i9_pdf` accepts
`include_full_ssn`; `submit_i9_section_1` accepts `ssn`; and `get_i9_form` now
returns `site_policy` with the two I-9 Settings switches, which is the read that
lets the wizard decide what to put on the wire. **Both gates are unchanged**: the
caller must ask, and the site must have `store_full_ssn` on. A site that never
opted in is *refused* — in the tool's own words, naming the switch — rather than
handed a blank comb, and the read still writes `full_ssn: true` into the audit row.

The old refusal argued the flag "needs a decision about the site's own retention
policy that belongs to an operator with the Desk in front of them." That is true,
and the conclusion did not follow: **that decision is `store_full_ssn`**, made in
the Desk, by an operator. The rest of the old argument — "the rendered page
leaves the box empty and the employee writes the number on it" — described a page
that gets *printed*. The app now seals and flattens the PDF with the captures
stamped into the page content. Nobody writes anything on it afterwards.

## 1a. The box these three lines share is 900 characters, and they overran it

Adding two entries to `Additional Information` broke something that had been safe
while three shared it. A form with a receipt, an E-Verify SSN note, two *located*
attestations and a fourth reverification came to **exactly** the 900-character
limit — and what fell off the end was `_overflow_note`, the line saying a
reverification is missing from the page. That is the single entry the module
calls the failure mode that matters most: a page that looks complete and is
missing the season somebody is asking about.

The limit is not geometry. The box is 309 x 116 points and `_prepare_widgets`
will shrink type to 4pt to make text fit, so 900 is a **legibility** ceiling and
raising it would only trade a dropped line for a grey smudge. Three changes
instead:

- **The prose is tighter.** The SSN line went from 274 characters to 164; the
  copies line dropped the document titles it was repeating from the boxes above.
- **The overflow note moved up**, ahead of the EIN, the copies and the caller's
  own lines — all of which are bookkeeping about facts printed elsewhere on the
  form or held on the record, and none of which is a missing reverification.
- **Truncation drops whole GROUPS** (`_fit`). The old rule sliced the joined body
  at 900 characters and appended a full stop, which produced sentences that
  stopped mid-clause and **ended in a full stop**: `"... records a receipt for
  a."` is a statement about a federal document that nobody wrote, and it looks
  finished.

Groups rather than lines, because a line-at-a-time rule orphans a heading — and
the first attempt at this fix did exactly that. `_overflow_note` opens by saying
two reverifications are missing and then lists them; keeping the opening and
dropping the list produced a page that **announced a list and showed none**,
which is a worse artefact than either the truncation or the omission. So the
opening and the entries are offered as separate groups: the statement survives, the
list is dropped first, and `_overflow_note`'s first line no longer claims the
extra entries "are listed here" — it says they are **not on this page** and where
to find them, which is true in both cases.

A later group may still fit when an earlier one did not: order decides priority,
not eligibility, so dropping the ~300-character overflow *list* does not also
drop the 25-character EIN behind it.

Worst realistic case now lands at **875/900** with the overflow statement intact.
The form this was reported from sits at 608 and loses nothing.

The three tests covering this were **vacuous on the first draft** — the fixture
used four reverifications, came to 844 characters, fit inside the budget, and
passed identically against the bug. Two of the assertions were weak in the same
way: the old character cut left `"Supplement B has"` in place while dropping the
entry it introduced, and the appended full stop satisfied an `endswith(".")`
check. The class now opens with `test_the_crowded_case_really_does_overrun`,
which is the control for every other test in it.

## 2. Every sealed I-9 said the signature was made in the Gulf of Guinea

This is the one that was false rather than silent:

    Coordinates 0.000000, 0.000000

`gps_latitude` and `gps_longitude` on `Signing Evidence` are **`Float`**, which
MariaDB stores `NOT NULL DEFAULT 0`. `signing_evidence.record` passes `None` for
a signature that reported no fix, Frappe casts it to `0.0` on insert, and
`pdf_seal._coordinates` guarded with:

    if latitude in (None, "") or longitude in (None, ""):

which is correct about the *argument* and never sees the value that comes back
out of the database. 0°N 0°E is open water about 300 miles off Ghana.

An inspector reading coordinates on a verification page has been told where
somebody stood. A record asserting more than it knows is the failure
`signing_evidence.py` opens by arguing against, and this was that failure printed
onto the artefact.

**Fixed at the reader, because it cannot be fixed at the writer.** A `Float`
column has no empty value that is not also a coordinate, so no sentinel would
help. `_coordinates` now treats the exact pair `(0, 0)` as unset and prints
nothing — the row is dropped entirely, not rendered as `Coordinates: —`, which
invites the reading that a fix was taken and lost.

**Only the exact pair.** A zero on one axis with a real value on the other is a
point on the equator or the prime meridian and is a real place. Refusing every
zero would have been a zero-drop of exactly the kind `test_zero_drop.py` exists
to catch, committed while fixing its mirror image.

**This is a consistency fix, not a new rule.** `tools/housing._gps` has guarded
the same pair the same way since it was written — `in (None, "")`, then
`if not float(latitude) and not float(longitude)` — and
`housing.check_coordinates` states the reasoning in the words this release keeps
reaching for: a record carrying half a coordinate "would sit on a map at
longitude zero — off the coast of Ghana, on the same meridian as everything else
anybody forgot to finish." `pdf_seal` is the module that did not do it.

**The standalone suite cannot reproduce this bug through normal seeding**, which
is why the test hands `_coordinates` two literal zeroes rather than seeding a
row. `STORE.seed` stores the dict it is given verbatim and does not model
`NOT NULL DEFAULT 0`, so a fixture seeding `gps_latitude=None` reads back `None`
in the suite and `0.0` on a bench. A test rewritten to look more realistic would
go vacuously green; the test says so in its docstring.

### And the I-9 now carries its own

`section_1_signed_gps` and `section_2_signed_gps`, beside the `_signed_ip`
columns they belong with, and printed in Additional Information beside the moment
and the address. **One `Data` column holding `"lat,lon"`, not two `Float`s** —
for the reason above: a string column has an empty value that means empty.

Both write paths fill them, through one helper (`args.as_gps`): the wizard's
`submit_i9_section_1` / `submit_i9_section_2`, and `signatures.py`'s
`SignatureBox`, which gains `signed_gps_field`. Written **only** where a
signature is, on the rule the moment and the address already follow — a location
on an attestation nobody made is a record of where something that did not happen
did not happen. Never blanked on a later signature that got no lock: a pad under
a tree must not erase a fix that was recorded.

`signed_gps_field` is also added to the document-fingerprint exclusion set. It is
written in the same breath as the image, so leaving it out would mean an employer
signing Section 2 changed a column covered by the fingerprint the worker's
Section 1 evidence row recorded that morning — the sealed page would have
reported the worker's own attestation as no longer matching what they attested to.

## 3. The document photographs were collected and went nowhere the form could see

The onboarding wizard photographs the List A or List B+C documents at the
tailgate and files them with `attach_onboarding_document`, which hangs them off
the **Employee**. That is the right home for the bytes — one upload path, one
permission check. But the I-9 itself held a `document_copies_stored` tickbox and
no way to answer *which* copies, or whether they are still there.

8 CFR 274a.2(b)(3): an employer who keeps copies must retain them **with** the
I-9 and produce them with it. A form that cannot name them cannot be produced
complete.

`list_a_doc_copy` / `list_b_doc_copy` / `list_c_doc_copy` are `Attach` columns on
I-9 Form, and `attach_onboarding_document` points the matching one at each
photograph **as it is filed**. Additional Information then records which lists
are retained:

> Document copies retained with this form: List B, List C (I-9 I9-2026-0011).

The list letter alone — the document title is printed in the Section 2 box three
inches above, and `Additional Information` is a shared budget (see below).

**Nothing is moved, re-uploaded or re-attached.** The File stays attached to the
Employee; the I-9 references it, exactly as `generated_pdf` and `signed_pdf` are
paths rather than bytes.

**The file name is never printed.** A `/private/files/...` path on a federal form
looks like something a reader can open and is not — it needs a session this app
does not hand out — and a retained I-9 outlives every URL scheme it was printed
under. The I-9's docname is what locates the copies for good, and it is on the
page already.

**It never raises.** The photograph has already landed and the worker has put
their passport away by the time the cross-reference runs; a failure here must not
read as a failed upload and send somebody back to photograph a passport twice.
The tool result carries `i9_form` when it linked and omits it when it did not.

**The Destroyed row is excluded by name.** `employee` is not unique on I-9 Form —
`destroy_i9` sets the status and *saves* rather than deleting, so a rehired
worker has two or more rows by construction. Linking a fresh photograph of a
current document onto the record that certifies its own disposal would
reconstitute part of a form the destruction certificate says is gone.

## What is NOT in this release

**The phone still sends four SSN digits.** Everything the server needs is here —
`site_policy` on `get_i9_form`, `ssn` on `submit_i9_section_1` — but the iOS
change that reads the policy and sends nine when the site asks is a client
release. Until it ships, a `store_full_ssn` site collects nine digits on the
handset and stores four, and the rendered page carries the prose line rather than
the comb.

**The phone still sends no GPS on the I-9 signature path.**
`OnboardingAPI.fileI9Signature` does not pass `latitude`/`longitude` to
`SignatureAPI.submit`, which has accepted them for several releases. The server
columns, the transport arguments and the rendering are all in place; the two
lines that populate them are in the client. **The seal page no longer claims a
location either way**, which is the half that was actively wrong.

**`_resolve_form` still picks Destroyed rows.** It resolves an I-9 from an
employee with a bare filter and no status exclusion, so a rehired worker's render
can refuse naming the wrong record. Flagged separately rather than fixed here —
it is a shared resolver on many tools and deserves its own pass.
