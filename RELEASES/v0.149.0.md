# v0.149.0 — the photograph reaches the books, and so does the coordinate

A photograph taken at a machine now appears in that machine's Attachments in the
Desk, on the ERPNext Asset, and not only on the tag record. The asset's GPS fix
travels with it and is kept current.

---

## What was actually happening, and what was not

The report was "I photographed a wind machine and a shed and the pictures
disappeared, because the sidecar stored them locally instead of pushing them to
ERPNext." Two thirds of that turns out not to be what the code does, and the
third that is real is worse than a lost photograph.

**Nothing is ever stored locally.** There is no SQLite anywhere in this app and
no local blob store. `stage_file_chunk` assembles a photograph into a private
Frappe `File` — ERPNext storage, verified by SHA-256 — and hands back its
docname. On the deployed site every one of the thirty-three valve photographs is
sitting there, private, attached to its `Asset Register` record.

**But the photograph only ever reached the TAG.** `_attach_photo` points the
File at `Asset Register`, which is where `export_insurance_schedule` reads
attachments from and where the Desk shows them — on a doctype most people never
open. Anybody looking at ERPNext's Asset for the machine saw nothing. That is
the half of the complaint that is real, and it is what this release fixes.

**The wind machine and the shed were never registered at all.** Not "registered
without a photograph" — absent. The site has thirty-three Asset Register rows
and every one is an Irrigation Valve. What it also has is one dead upload
session from 11:00 the previous morning: one chunk of three, 512 KB of 1,296,901,
abandoned mid-flight.

The handset uploads the photograph BEFORE it calls `register_asset`, and a
photograph is required to submit at all. When the upload dies on a thin field
link, the throw takes the whole registration with it and no asset is created.
Two buildings were photographed and neither the picture nor the machine reached
the site. **That ordering is in the iOS app and is not changed here** — the note
at the end says what would fix it.

## The photograph is copied, not moved

Every photograph filed against a tag is now also filed against the ERPNext
Asset, through Frappe's own `File.create_attachment_copy` — "efficiently copy an
attachment from one document to another by reusing `file_url`". It sets
`flags.copy_from_existing_file`, which skips re-writing the bytes and keeps the
rest of the insert lifecycle.

**One blob, two rows.** The photograph appears in the Attachments sidebar of the
tag AND of the Asset, and the megabyte is stored once.

**A copy rather than a move, deliberately.** `export_insurance_schedule` reads
the attachments on the Asset Register; taking the photograph away to put it on
the Asset would empty an insurance schedule to fill a sidebar.

**Deleting either row is safe.** Frappe's `_delete_file_on_disk` removes the blob
only when no other File row shares its `content_hash`. That guarantee is why two
rows is the right shape and a shared `file_url` with one row would not have been.

**A tag costed later brings its photographs with it.** A valve tagged and
photographed in the field has no purchase value, so no Asset exists yet. When
somebody prices it at the desk weeks later, the Asset is created then and the
photograph taken in the field travels to it. Without that, the only assets with
pictures would be the ones somebody happened to price on the spot.

**It does not duplicate.** Every edit to a tag runs the mirror; copying
unconditionally would give an Asset a fresh copy of every photograph each time
somebody fixed a typo. Already-present URLs are skipped.

`erpnext_asset_photos` on the reply counts what the call filed — a count and not
a silence, because the complaint this answers is "the picture went nowhere" and
an answer that does not say what happened to it cannot settle that.

## The coordinate, and why it is the one deliberate second copy

`gps_latitude` and `gps_longitude` are now custom fields on ERPNext's Asset,
written on creation and **rewritten on every sync**.

v0.148.0 argued against exactly this — twenty operational columns duplicated
onto the Asset are a shadow layer, and two editable copies of one coordinate
will disagree. That argument still holds for the serial number, the model, the
service schedule and the hour meter, all of which stay on the tag and are
reached through the Link.

It does not hold for position, because the unified map plots equipment out of
the fixed-asset register alongside blocks, zones and valves — and a map that had
to join through `Asset Register` to find a tractor could not plot one somebody
created in the Desk, which is half the machines on a farm that also buys through
invoices. The drift a second copy invites is answered by refreshing it on every
sync and stamping when that happened, not by hoping.

**The coordinate is refreshed and the money is not**, and the two live one line
apart in `_refresh` on purpose. A price is a fact about a transaction that
happened once. A position is a fact about where the thing is standing now, and
it is read by somebody trying to walk to it.

**Zero is not a position.** A Frappe Float is `NOT NULL DEFAULT 0`, so a tag
with no fix reads exactly 0.0 and there is no null to tell it from a reading.
Null Island is 1,600 km off the coast of Ghana. Both columns are written or
neither is, and a cleared fix never overwrites a good one — a machine that had a
position does not lose it to a sync.

That last property is what the test asserts, and the first version of the test
did not. `assertFalse(latitude)` cannot tell "nothing was written" from "a zero
was written", because a Float column answers 0.0 either way; it passed under a
mutation that let zeros through. What is observable, and what is the actual
harm, is the pin moving out of the orchard and into the Atlantic.

## The upload path, traced end to end

Asked for, and worth writing down because the shape is not what it looks like:

```
iOS  EvidenceImageProcessor.prepare(photo)          a JPEG in memory
  →  ChunkUploader.uploadAttachment(...)
  →  POST erpnext_mcp.api.files.stage_file_chunk    base64, 512 KB a slice
  →  POST erpnext_mcp.api.files.finalize_staged_file  SHA-256 verified
       ← { file_token: "<File docname>" }           a private Frappe File
  →  POST erpnext_mcp.api.mobile.register_asset
       { ..., photo_file_token: "<File docname>" }
server  _attach_photo   points that File at the Asset Register row
        asset_mirror.copy_photographs  copies it onto the ERPNext Asset
        asset_mirror  sets Asset.image to the same file_url
```

**No multipart body and no base64 crosses the asset call**, and nothing here
decodes an image. The bytes went up through the chunked path and already are an
ERPNext File; what `register_asset` receives is that File's docname. So there
was no format to translate and none was added — the iOS contract is untouched,
which is also why no argument on any mobile route changed in this release.

`/api/method/upload_file` is deliberately not used. It is the one path the
Tailscale funnel strips the credential from, which is the reason the chunked
route exists at all; the iOS source says so in three places.

## What is still open, and it is the part that lost the machines

A photograph that fails to upload still takes the whole registration with it,
because the handset uploads first and requires a photograph to submit. The
server already supports the other order — `register_asset` takes no photograph
at all, and `attach_file_to_document` files one afterwards, which is exactly what
the app's own `retryPhotoAttach` already calls. Reordering `submit()` to register
first and attach second would turn "lost the wind machine" into "registered the
wind machine, retry the photo". **That is an iOS change and is not in this
release.**

The one abandoned upload session is still on the site and still resumable —
`list_staged_uploads` has it with the missing slice indexes, and
`cancel_staged_upload` clears it. It expires 24 hours after its last activity.

## Also in this release

**The harness models `File.create_attachment_copy`** and now lists `Asset.image`,
which it did not — so `compat.has_field("Asset", "image")` answered False and the
thumbnail assignment was a silent no-op the suite could not see.

**`callable(...)` rather than `hasattr(...)`** on the copy helper. Frappe's own
Document has no `__getattr__`, so `hasattr` is right on a bench — and the
standalone double's returns None for every unknown key, making `hasattr` True for
a method that does not exist. A guard that is always True would have made the
fallback branch unreachable in the only place it can be tested.
