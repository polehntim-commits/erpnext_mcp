# v0.150.0 — a building drawn as a building, on one map with everything else

One tool, six columns on `Asset Register`, one on ERPNext's Asset, and one
optional flag on the map read every handset already calls.

---

## Two halves of one thing

**`set_asset_boundary`** gives an asset a FOOTPRINT — the outline a shed, a pump
house, a cabin or a cold store occupies.

**`list_field_boundaries(include_assets=true)`** then returns blocks, parcels,
equipment and buildings in one answer, each row saying whether it draws as a pin
or an outline.

Before this, the asset register held a single coordinate per asset and the map
read held only ground. A dispatcher looking for the tractor and the shed was
looking at a different screen from the one with the blocks on it.

## Why a building needs a shape and not just a dot

A pin says where to walk. An outline says what is there: which way the shop
faces, whether the cabin row sits inside the parcel, how much of the yard the
cold store already takes, which side of the shed the pad is on. For a shed, a
pump house or a cabin the footprint IS the fact, and a map that draws every
building as an identical dot cannot answer any of it.

## The same six columns as every other boundary on this site

`Field`, `Parcel` and `Irrigation Zone` each carry `boundary_geojson` plus five
values derived from it — centroid, bounding box, H3 cells at five resolutions,
computed area. `Asset Register` now carries the same six, produced by the same
`geo.derive`, and read back by the same `geo.stored_shape`.

A seventh shape stored a seventh way would need its own reader in every
consumer. None of the derived values is an argument and all are read-only: a
figure a person can change independently of the polygon is a figure that will
disagree with it.

**A self-intersecting trace is refused.** A bow tie has an area a computer will
happily report and a containment test nobody can trust, and it is exactly what a
hand-walked trace with two vertices swapped produces.

**An outline on a valve is warned about and stored, not refused.** Guessing
which asset types a farm is allowed to trace is how a generator pad becomes
unrecordable. But a polygon on a tractor is almost certainly the wrong asset, so
it gets a sentence saying so.

**The recorded pin is checked against the outline and reported.** A fix taken at
the door of a shed is a metre outside it; one taken from across the yard is
fifty. Refusing either would make a real building unrecordable; saying nothing
would leave a pin and an outline disagreeing on a map with nobody told which to
believe.

## One map, everything on it

`include_assets` is opt in for the reason parcels are: five hundred valves is
five hundred rows a caller drawing only blocks has no use for.

| Row key | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `geometry` | `"polygon"` or `"point"` — which one this asset draws as |
| `boundary_geojson` | the outline, when there is one |
| `gps_latitude`, `gps_longitude` | the pin, when there is one |
| `centroid_lat`, `centroid_lon` | where to centre or label, from whichever exists |
| `asset_type`, `description`, `current_state` | what it is and what it is doing |

Plus `asset_count`, `asset_polygon_count` and `asset_unplaced_count` on the
answer.

**`geometry` is a property of the asset, not a choice the caller makes.** A
valve, a tractor and a sprayer have a position; a shed, a pump house, a cabin
and a cold store have a footprint. Saying which per row is what lets a client
draw a marker or a shape without knowing this app's asset vocabulary — and a
building nobody has traced yet falls back to its pin rather than vanishing off
the map.

**Retired assets are left off.** A map is a picture of what is out there now,
and a decommissioned pump drawn beside a working one is a dispatch sent to the
wrong machine.

**An asset with neither a fix nor an outline is left off AND COUNTED.** A Frappe
Float is `NOT NULL DEFAULT 0`, so every asset tagged before anybody switched GPS
on reads exactly 0.0 — indistinguishable from a reading. Null Island is 1,600 km
off the coast of Ghana. Plotting those puts the farm's whole register in the Gulf
of Guinea; dropping them silently makes a map that is missing half the valves and
does not say so. `asset_unplaced_count` is the third answer.

## The footprint reaches the books too

`boundary_geojson` joins `gps_latitude` and `gps_longitude` as a column on
ERPNext's Asset, written and refreshed by the same mirror. Same argument as
v0.149.0 made for the coordinate: the map plots out of the fixed-asset register,
and one that had to join through `Asset Register` could not draw a building
somebody created in the Desk.

An asset that is not on the books keeps its footprint anyway — the outline is a
fact about the ground whether or not anybody has priced the building.

## What this does NOT do, and it is the honest half

**iOS cannot draw a building outline yet.** `StructurePinView` drops a POINT: a
person picks House, Cabin, Shop or Barn and taps a spot. There is no trace-the-
walls UI, and `MapOverlay.LayerKey` is a fixed Swift enum, so a client that has
not been changed decodes the map answer it already knows and ignores `assets`
entirely.

So what ships here is the whole server half — somewhere to put a footprint, a
validated way to put one there, and a map read that returns it — and nothing on
the handset changes until the app adds the capture and the layer. `iOS must not
change` was the instruction; this is what that instruction costs, stated rather
than papered over.

**What works today without any iOS change:** `set_asset_boundary` through the
MCP endpoint, the outline and the derived values visible on the Asset Register
form in the Desk, `boundary_geojson` on the ERPNext Asset, and the
`include_assets` payload for any client that asks for it.

**No mobile route was added for the boundary.** `set_field_boundary`,
`set_zone_boundary` and `set_parcel_boundary` each have one because iOS's
`BoundaryRecordingModel` calls them. Nothing calls a structure equivalent yet,
and publishing a route to every enrolled handset for a caller that does not
exist is surface without a user. It is one wrapper when the app is ready.
