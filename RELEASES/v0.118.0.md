# v0.118.0 — Farm App Retirement, Cycle 1

The Flask sidecar is being retired into this app. Cycle 1 builds the five
registers it held that this one did not, ports the two things in that codebase
that could not be re-derived from its schema, and adds one field that is not a
migration at all.

Eight DocTypes, thirty-three tools (eighteen read, fifteen mutating), two salvage
modules, 186 tests. No existing tool signature changed.

---

## What was actually missing

The migration plan lists sixteen gaps across three cycles. Cycle 1 is the five
that are **data models** — the ones that have to exist before any data can move —
plus one new feature and the salvage.

## The block ticker

The only thing here that is not a migration.

A ticker is the **buyer's** name for a block — `YC-3` for Yellow Camp Block 3. It
goes on a purchase order, comes back on a settlement, and is how a buyer asks for
the same fruit next season without knowing or caring what the docname is.

It is deliberately not `block_number`. A block number is what the crew and the
old records call it, is duplicated across parcels on purpose, and changes when
somebody re-splits a block. A ticker is a promise made to somebody **outside** the
business, so:

- **unique across the company**, not across the parcel — the whole point is that a
  buyer can say it without knowing which parcel the block sits on;
- **folded to upper case before the uniqueness check**, not after. `yc-3` and
  `YC-3` reaching the check as different strings would let both exist, and the
  duplicate would be discovered by a buyer receiving the wrong block's fruit;
- **ten characters**, the width of a column on a printed settlement sheet;
- **empty is the normal state.** Most blocks are never sold by name, and treating
  `''` as a value would let the first untickered block lock out every other one.

### Why the Planting Season copy is a copy

`Planting Season.block_ticker` is written by the controller on save, not fetched.

A `fetch_from` shows whatever the Field says *today*. Re-lettering a block in 2027
would then silently relabel its 2024 season, and the 2024 settlements that quoted
the old code would stop agreeing with the season record they were settled
against. What the buyer called the block that year is part of what happened.

It also could not have worked: the standalone double has no `fetch_from` at all,
so the column would have been null in every test and populated on a bench — or
the reverse, which is worse.

## IoT

### `last_seen` means the device spoke

The register's whole value is telling a dry block from a dead probe. A soil
sensor silent for a week is **not reading zero moisture** — it is not reading, and
a block gets irrigated or does not on the difference.

So `create_iot_reading` is the only writer of that column, and
`update_iot_device` **refuses the argument**. A `last_seen` somebody could type is
the one field that would make a dead sensor look alive.

`online` is computed at read time and stored nowhere, for the matching reason: a
stored flag is wrong from the moment a device goes quiet, and nothing runs to
update it — which is the only moment it matters. `health_warnings` says which of
four things is wrong, in the words somebody would need: never reported, silent for
so many hours, battery low, never calibrated. "Never reported" is kept separate
from "gone quiet" because the first is almost always a registration that was never
finished and the second is a battery, a radio or a tractor.

### A reading keeps the block its device sat in

Denormalised at write time and never resolved again. Through a link, a probe moved
from Block 3 to Block 7 in July would retroactively move every June reading with
it — and the June irrigation decisions those readings justified would no longer be
defensible from the record.

The timestamp is the **device's** and is required rather than defaulted to now: a
device posting a buffered backlog would otherwise have every reading in it stamped
with the moment its radio came back, which is the one time they did not happen.
Buffered readings are exactly the ones somebody wants during a frost event.

Duplicates on `(device, reading_type, timestamp)` are refused — the same
constraint the SQLite table carried. Devices retry, gateways replay, and a batch
stored twice doubles every average computed off it.

### Aggregates never cross a unit boundary

`get_device_readings` summarises per reading type **and per unit**. A device
reporting both soil temperature and soil moisture has two series, and a single
mean over the pair is a number with no referent. Where one type arrives in two
units — a device reconfigured mid-season — `mixed_units` says so and the figures
carry a note, rather than a mean being taken through the change.

`quality` is a column and not a filter applied before storage. A reading the
gateway thought was wrong is still what the sensor said, and the run of suspect
values either side of a real event is often the only evidence it happened.

## Competitive intelligence

### Three registers because they move at three speeds

A participant is a standing fact about an organisation, revised yearly. A move is
a dated event, written once. A target is a decision in progress with money
attached, touched weekly while a deal is live. Collapsing any two means the slow
one is rewritten every time the fast one moves.

### The four fit scores fail separately

A financially distressed neighbour with perfect strategic fit and no cultural fit
is a deal that closes and then does not work — and a single attractiveness number
hides exactly that case.

`accretive_score` is their mean, derived on save and **refused as an argument**,
with `weakest_dimension` reported beside it. A deal fails on its weakest score
rather than on its average, and the mean is what a list sorts on.

An **unscored** target scores `null`, not zero. Zero is an answer; an unassessed
target must not sort beside one assessed as worthless.

`live_count` is reported separately from the total, because a pipeline of forty
targets of which thirty-five are Closed or Passed is a pipeline of five and the
count that gets quoted is the wrong one by default.

### Why the land, water and varietal values are broken out

A payback period of eleven years disqualifies a deal in most businesses and is
unremarkable in tree fruit, where the asset is land and water and the buyer is
thinking in a horizon measured in plantings — hence a 25-year default horizon.
`asset_value_total` is reported against the going-concern estimate, because the
two disagreeing is the interesting case: an asset total above the estimated value
is a target worth buying for the ground rather than for the business.

Water rights are their own line because in much of the interior West they are the
largest single number on the page and do not move with the orchard. Varietal IP is
its own line because club rights do not always transfer with a sale, and finding
that out after closing is the classic way that line becomes zero.

### Nothing here decides

`assess_acquisition_target`, `rank_acquisition_targets` and
`generate_competitive_landscape` are the part deliberately **not** ported. The
scoring is a judgement a person makes; the arithmetic on it happens in one place;
what a landscape *means* is what the operator is being paid to work out.

What the tools do instead is put the evidence in front of them.
`urgent_unanswered` on the move register is the gap between what was recommended
and what was actually done — invisible move by move, and the most instructive
thing in the file. The farm_app's landscape prompt is preserved as data.

## Strategy

### Superseded, never edited into the next one

Naming a `previous_version` versions the new plan and retires its predecessor in
one call, leaving the old wording exactly as written. The interesting question
about a strategy is almost always what it *used* to say: a farm that changed its
mind about a market in 2025 needs the 2024 wording to understand why.

`version` is derived and refused as an argument — a number somebody types is one
that will eventually be typed twice, and two plans both calling themselves v3 make
the chain unorderable. A circular chain is refused for the same reason: a loop
leaves "what did we say before this" with no answer.

Retiring the predecessor is a **side effect on save**, which is a real decision.
The alternative is asking the author to go and edit the old plan too — and on the
occasions they do not, it stays Implemented for ever, which reads as two live
strategies.

### An objective is its own doctype

The migration plan called for a child table. It is a standalone doctype here, for
two reasons:

1. Recording this quarter's actual would otherwise be a **write to the strategy
   document** — re-stamping it, and putting a numbers entry in the same audit
   trail as a change of direction.
2. "Show me everything overdue across every plan" becomes one query rather than a
   walk through every parent.

Target and actual are free text because half of these are not numbers —
`14 tons/acre`, `two new buyers`, `crew housed on site`. A Float column would
silently exclude the ones that matter most and that nobody would then track
anywhere.

An **undated actual is refused**: a figure with no date cannot be told from one
taken eighteen months ago, so it is either believed and wrong or discounted and
useless. `Achieved` with an empty actual is refused too — the most flattering row
a plan can carry and the one nobody can check.

`achieved_rate` is computed over **settled** objectives only; counting ones still
in progress flatters every plan on the day it is written. `overdue` means past its
date **and still open**, so a `Failed` objective does not sit on the list for ever.

## Residue limits

### `get_mrl_for_chemical_crop_market` never guesses

A miss returns `found: false`. It will not fall back to another market's figure,
will not average across markets, and will not offer the nearest crop. Every one of
those returns something that **looks like an answer** to a question about whether
a load can ship.

What comes back instead is the neighbouring evidence, labelled as research: the
same ingredient's limits in other markets, and the same market's limits on other
ingredients. That is material for a person; it is not a substitute for the answer.

### The source is required

A load is refused at a border against a named regulation on a named date, and "we
had 0.5 written down" is not a defence. A tier-4 inferred limit with an honest
note is worth keeping; a bare number with no provenance is **worse than nothing**,
because it looks identical to a checked one.

`source_tier` records how far from the official register the figure was found —
1 official database, 2 government gazette, 3 industry or academic cross-reference,
4 inferred — and `official_source` says it in words rather than leaving a digit a
caller has to know how to read.

`chemical` is the **active ingredient**, not the trade name: several products
share one active ingredient and the limit attaches to the ingredient.

### Zero is a limit; a ban is not

A non-detect requirement is the strictest MRL there is, and treating zero as
missing would convert it into no limit at all. A banned substance still carries a
default figure and the load is refused on the ban **regardless of the residue
found**, so the two are separate columns and the warning says which is doing the
work.

`expiry_date` is a **re-check** date rather than the regulation's own lapse — the
first is the useful one. `needs_recheck` is what the register exists for: limits
are revised, harmonised and withdrawn constantly, and a stale one is more
dangerous than a missing one because nobody goes looking.

## The salvage

### `ipm_reference.py` — 405 rows of literature

Ported out of `seed_ipm_data.py`, `seed_pests_beneficials.py` and
`app/data/pesticide_labels_data.py`, with the sources named.

| Table | Rows |
| --- | --- |
| `pest_models` | 28 |
| `beneficials` | 19 |
| `pest_damage` | 8 |
| `beneficial_activity` | 10 |
| `pesticide_efficacy` | 24 |
| `beneficial_toxicity` | **80** |
| `pesticide_products` | 46 |
| `pesticide_labels` | 190 |

This is **not** a seeder. Everything in it is a published fact about an organism
or a product; none of it is a fact about anybody's farm, so none of it belongs in
a DocType an operator owns and corrects. `agronomy_seed.py` lays down the starting
book of things a farm edits; this is the opposite kind of data, read and never
written. `get_ipm_reference` touches no doctype at all and works on a bench with
nothing installed.

**The toxicity table is why it was worth carrying.** Every label states a PHI and
an REI because the law requires it; almost none states what the product does to
the predators already working the block. A farm that sprays a pyrethroid for one
aphid flush and loses its predatory mites has bought a spider mite outbreak in
August, and that consequence is written down almost nowhere else.

`rotation_partners` excludes same-group alternatives rather than ranking them
lower: two products that work equally well and share an IRAC group are one product
as far as resistance is concerned, and including them would let a rotation be
built out of one chemistry.

**Matching is exact apart from case and spacing.** The farm_app had a fuzzy pest
matcher and it was a persistent source of rows quietly attached to the wrong
organism. `Cherry Slug` and `Pear Slug` are the same animal and `Spider Mites` is
two species; a matcher confident enough to bridge those is confident enough to
bridge things that must not be bridged. A miss returns nothing, which a caller can
see.

### `prompt_templates.py` — 13 prompts, as data

The one part of the Flask app that cannot be re-derived from the schema. These are
not a description of the data — they are two years of somebody discovering, one
bad answer at a time, what a model has to be told before it stops inventing MRLs
and starts citing them.

`mrl_research_single` names **sixteen national regulators** and a four-tier
fallback ladder, with an eight-step decision tree, for exactly that reason: a
model asked for an MRL returns `NOT_FOUND` at the first miss unless it is given an
explicit ladder to climb and told that inference *with its tier recorded* beats
silence.

The pest family's recurring device is the **numbered name list**, and it is not
decoration: each of those prompts writes into a table keyed by an existing pest,
beneficial or product name, and a model returning "codling moths" for
`Codling Moth` produces a row that silently attaches to nothing.

**Nothing renders them into a writer.** `utils/ai_call.py` — the provider dispatch
to Ollama, xAI and Anthropic — is not ported: an MCP server is already on the
other end of a model, so a tool that renders one of these and hands it back is the
whole integration. A generated strategic plan or an unreviewed MRL landing in a
register is a document the farm is measured against that nobody chose, and the
entire value of the `source` column is that a person put something in it.

The payroll-tax and GAAP-advice prompts are left behind on purpose. This app
already computes withholding from tables an operator can inspect, and a model
asked to be authoritative about somebody's tax position is a liability rather than
a feature. What is kept here is the set whose answers a person reviews before
anything is written down.

## The bug the tests found

All four new tool modules staged their changes with
`str(before or "") != str(after or "")` — the obvious spelling, and one that
**silently drops zero**, since `0 or ""` is `""`.

Setting an MRL of 0 ppm or a cultural fit score of 0 over an empty column staged
nothing, the write was lost, and the only symptom was a required field the caller
believed they had supplied. It surfaced as `MRL (ppm) is required` on a call that
passed `mrl_ppm: 0`.

Fixed with an explicit `_same` that tells blank from zero. Both regressions are
asserted, and they are the two values most worth recording: a non-detect residue
limit is the strictest limit there is, and a cultural fit of zero is the score
that most often decides a deal.

claim `get_mrl_for_chemical_crop_market` makes.

## Not in this release

Cycle 2 (utility ports, satellite, SQLite migration scripts) and Cycle 3 (iOS
route cutover, Umbrel container removal) are untouched.

`MRL Research Session` from the plan is **not** a separate doctype. Its
provenance columns — `research_notes`, `research_response`, `source_tier`,
`confidence`, `substance_status` — are on `MRL Record` itself, so the research
trail lives with the answer it produced rather than in a second register that has
to be joined to it.
