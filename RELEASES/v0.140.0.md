# v0.140.0 — the shift nobody closed, and the block nobody was told about

Two things that were already known by this server and were not reaching the one
person who could act on them.

---

## 1. The horn on a heat crossing

### What was already true

The weather sweep has run every fifteen minutes since v0.19.4. On a shift that is
open and has coordinates, it fetches the conditions, appends an immutable reading
to the shift's `weather_timeline`, and — the first time a reading is at or above
the threshold — writes a **Threshold Crossed** compliance event onto the shift.

That event says: *the heat index reached 84 °F at 11:45.*

```
  before                              after
  ─────────────────────────────       ─────────────────────────────
  11:45  sweep reads 84 °F            11:45  sweep reads 84 °F
  11:45  reading appended             11:45  reading appended
  11:45  Threshold Crossed logged     11:45  Threshold Crossed logged
         …                            11:45  the foreman's phone rings
  13:20  foreman opens the app                with the block, both
         at lunch and sees it                 numbers, and a button
```

OAR 437-004-1131 attaches its obligations **at the crossing** — water at the
required rate, shade within reach, the preventative cool-down cycle, observation
for heat illness signs. An inspector asking whether relief was provided in time is
asking about the crossing. The gap between 11:45 and lunch was not a gap in the
evidence; it was a gap in the only thing the evidence is for.

### Who it goes to, and why that is one named person

**The shift's own foreman.** This is the only push in this app addressed to a
single named individual rather than to a role or to a crew, and the reasoning is
not new to this release — the compliance rule of the same name already says it in
code: `producer_assigned_to_expression` is `row.foreman`.

- **Not the office.** `alert_payload` goes to everybody holding a dispatch role,
  which is right for an expiring I-9 — the worker cannot act on it, the employer
  can. Here the person who can act is the one standing in the block. A push to
  the office reaches people who cannot call a break in a field they are not in.
- **Not the crew.** A break horn goes to twenty phones because a break is twenty
  people's news. This is one person's *decision*, and a crew buzzed about
  decisions they cannot make stops reading the horn that tells them to stop work.
- **A shift with no foreman falls back to the supervisors and says which
  happened.** That is an imported or hand-written shift, and the choice there is
  between the wrong recipients and nobody at all.

### It pierces Do Not Disturb, which nothing else new does

`INTERRUPTION_ACTIVE` was added in v0.107.0 with an argument attached: a server
that overrides a foreman's Focus **nightly** is silenced by the second week, and
the break horn goes with it. A dispatched task and a compliance alert raised by a
02:00 sweep therefore get `active` and priority 5.

This gets `time-sensitive` and priority 10, and the thing that makes that
defensible is the fence below. A shift crosses **once**. A foreman pierced by this
is pierced on the hot days and on no others.

### The fence is not the one the timeline uses

`already_crossed` asks whether the shift carries *any* Threshold Crossed event.
That is exactly right for the timeline — one row per shift is what stops nine
hours above eighty degrees becoming thirty-six identical rows that bury the water
breaks underneath them.

It is exactly wrong for the horn, because of a case that reads like a corner and
is not:

> A Spray shift crosses the **wind** threshold at 09:00 in cool air. It gets its
> one Threshold Crossed event. The afternoon turns hot. `already_crossed` says
> yes — and the phone never rings on the hottest day of the season, for a reason
> nobody would ever find.

So `weather.heat_announced_for` reads the **stored snapshot numbers** on each
existing event — `weather_snapshot_temp_f` and `weather_snapshot_heat_index_f`,
against the same company thresholds the crossing itself was judged on — and asks
whether this shift has already announced *heat*. Not the description text, which
is a sentence somebody may reword.

Wind is not pushed at all. An applicator over the wind limit knows from the boom;
heat is the one where the condition is survivable minute to minute and the
obligation on it is not.

**A backfill never rings anybody.** `backfill_weather_for_shift` reads the archive
and does not go through `evaluate_thresholds` — it never did. A phone that buzzes
about Tuesday on Thursday teaches its owner to ignore the one that buzzes about
now.

### What the handset gets

```json
{
  "aps": {
    "alert": {"title": "Heat threshold crossed",
              "body": "Block 7 North — 92°F / 102°F heat index. Call the cool-down and log it."},
    "sound": "default",
    "interruption-level": "time-sensitive",
    "category": "FARM_HEAT"
  },
  "shift": "SHIFT-2026-0114",
  "phase": "heat",
  "location": "Block 7 North",
  "temp_f": 92.4, "ambient_temp_f": 92.4, "heat_index_f": 101.7,
  "reading_datetime": "2026-08-26 13:45:00",
  "threshold_temp_f": 80.0, "threshold_heat_index_f": 80.0,
  "action": {"open": "shift", "shift": "SHIFT-2026-0114",
             "then": "log_break", "endpoint": "log_shift_break",
             "break_kind": "Cool-Down"}
}
```

Both temperatures are **in the body and not only in the custom keys**: the lock
screen is where "do I stop the crew" is decided, and a key the app must be opened
to read is not on the lock screen. `action` names a route this app actually
publishes — `log_shift_break` is a real mobile endpoint and `Cool-Down` is a real
`BREAK_KINDS` entry, asserted against both. A payload naming a screen invented for
the payload would be a contract with nobody on the other end of it.
`ambient_temp_f` carries the same number under the name the iOS heat-break payload
reads, from one value, the way `shifts.describe_event_row` already does.

It does **not** spend a break tone. The two `.caf` files mean "stop work" and
"resume" to a whole crew; this reaches one phone and asks its owner to decide.

### Nothing it does can cost the reading

No p8 key, no enrolled handset, a transport that 503s, a shift with no foreman —
every one is a **named report** beside a timeline that was written anyway. The
report is surfaced on `fetch_weather_now` as `heat_push`, and the key is
**absent** rather than zeroed when no horn was attempted, so "this shift had
already crossed" stays distinguishable from "the horn reached nobody".

## 2. `end_stale_shift` no longer requires an end time

### What changed

`end_datetime` was required. It is now optional, and omitting it credits **the
start plus eight hours, clamped so it can never reach past now**.

### Why the old refusal was right, and why it does not apply

The refusal's own sentence made the case against a default of `now`:

> A crew that stopped work at 14:00 on Tuesday and is being clocked out on
> Thursday morning would be credited with forty hours, and every one of those
> hours would reach their Attendance row and their pay.

That is an argument against a default that **grows with the delay** — and how long
nobody noticed is precisely the quantity this tool selects its subjects on. The
worse the neglect, the wronger the number, without limit.

Eight hours does not grow at all:

```
  shift found after…      default of now       default of start + 8h
  ──────────────────      ──────────────       ─────────────────────
  20 hours                20.0 paid hours      8.0 paid hours
  120 hours               120.0 paid hours     8.0 paid hours
```

A crew that worked six is overpaid by two rather than by thirty-four. A crew that
worked ten is **underpaid** by two — which is the direction somebody notices, and
therefore the direction that gets corrected.

**The clamp is not decoration.** `stale_after_hours` is an argument, so an
operation running short shifts may fence at four — and start-plus-eight on a
five-hour-old shift is three hours in the future, which this tool's own future
check would refuse. Clamped, the assumption is "a working day, or as much of one
as has actually elapsed", which is true in both cases.

### It says it was an assumption

`end_datetime_assumed: true` and `assumed_shift_hours` in the answer, an
`end_time_note` explaining what it reached, `; end time ASSUMED (8h from start)`
on the summary, and a sentence on the shift's own `foreman_notes` beside the
`ADMINISTRATIVE CLOSE` line — because the answer is read once and the document is
read by whoever asks later. An assumption that reads like a measurement is the
only version of this that would be dishonest.

A stated `end_datetime` is still the right answer, is still what a caller who
knows should send, and is flagged as nothing at all.

### One bug found on the way

`foreman_notes` was accepted as an argument and then **silently discarded** on any
shift that already carried notes — `doc.foreman_notes or as_str(args,
"foreman_notes")` takes the first truthy one, and a shift with any history has
notes. An argument accepted and then dropped is worse than one refused. Every line
is kept now: the shift's own notes, the caller's, and the administrative-close
sentence.

## What is not in this release

**No new tool.** `end_stale_shift` shipped in v0.131.0 and this changes one of its
arguments. **No new doctype and no migration.** The heat push reads columns that
have existed since v0.19.4 and writes nothing.

**No compliance record is filed by any of this.** A notification is not a record
and does not claim to be one. `evaluate_thresholds` still refuses to create a Heat
Exposure Event from a temperature, for the reason it always has: that record names
which crew was exposed, what water was provided, whether the rest cycle was taken
and whether anybody showed signs, and it carries a supervisor's signature. Five
judgements by the person who was standing there. The horn asks them to look.

## Tests

`tests_standalone/test_heat_push.py` — 27 tests, seven claims, with the two
negative controls the design turns on:

- `test_a_wind_crossing_does_not_spend_the_shifts_heat_horn` fails if the fence is
  `already_crossed` rather than `heat_announced_for`. Verified by making that
  substitution.
- `test_a_second_hot_reading_does_not_ring_again` fails if the fence is removed.
  Verified.

`tests_standalone/test_crew_view.py` — `TheAssumedEndTime`, 9 tests, whose load-
bearing one is `test_the_default_does_not_grow_with_how_long_nobody_noticed`: the
same close against a 20-hour shift and a 120-hour shift must produce the same
paid hours. A default of `now` passes every other test in the class and fails
that one. Verified by making that substitution.

`test_the_end_time_is_required_and_never_defaulted` was removed, because the
behaviour it asserted is the behaviour this release deliberately changes.
